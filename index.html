Six telltale signs that a site was generated rather than hand-crafted. Learn them, then test yourself.

The telltale signs

None of these prove anything alone. Together, they paint a picture.

1. The purple-to-pink gradient

Indigo, violet and pink gradients on headlines, buttons and glowing backgrounds are a very common default look.

Look for: gradient text on the main headline.

2. Three cards, three emojis

A row of three identical feature cards, each topped with an emoji or a simple icon in a rounded square.

Look for: "Fast", "Secure", "Responsive" as the three titles.

3. Vague, polished copy

Headlines like "Build something beautiful" or "Elevate your workflow" sound nice but say very little about what the product actually does.

Look for: big promises, no specifics.

4. Pill buttons and soft everything

Fully rounded buttons, rounded cards, soft shadows and a button that lifts slightly on hover.

Look for: no sharp corners anywhere.

5. Perfectly symmetric layout

Centered hero, evenly spaced sections, matching card heights. Human designers usually break the grid somewhere on purpose.

Look for: zero personality quirks.

6. Generic placeholders

Names like "John Doe", fake testimonials, "Acme Inc.", stock-looking images or "Your text here" left in the page.

Look for: content that could belong to any company.

A fair warning

Spotting AI is not an exact science.

Plenty of human designers use gradients, rounded corners and three-column layouts too. AI tools learned those patterns from real websites, after all.

And a skilled person can prompt, edit and polish an AI-built site until it looks completely unique. Treat this list as clues, not proof.
